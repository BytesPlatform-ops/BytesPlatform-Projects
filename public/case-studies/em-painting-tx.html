The business

The job is local, visual and high-intent. The site has to prove all three

E&M Commercial and Residential Painting is a Houston-area painting contractor. The home page title states the ambition: “Top Houston Painters | Commercial & Residential Painting.” Behind it sit five service pages, three luxury pages, a set of city pages, a gallery of roughly forty project photographs and a footer naming eighteen service areas from Galveston to The Woodlands.

Reduce that to “we paint houses” and the business disappears into the most crowded local category there is. The published scope is wider: office buildings, apartment complexes, condominium and townhouse associations, schools, churches and warehouses on the commercial side; cabinets, trim, shutters, barns and playhouses on the residential side. The site cannot win on a sentence every contractor writes. It has to answer the three things a buyer checks with a page each.

Two buyers, two burdens of proof

  • →A homeowner reading for workmanship: will they protect my floors, can I see a kitchen they did.
  • →A property manager reading for scope: have they done an apartment complex, what standing do they show.
  • →One week of comparing three estimates, with the site needing to be one of the two called back.

The challenge

Five services, two buyers, a whole metro area

Residential and commercial, interior and exterior, standard and luxury, across a metro area, bought by people who have already searched for three competitors. Five pressures shaped the build.

The catalogue is broad, the need is narrow

Interior, exterior, cabinets, commercial and luxury. A buyer wants one of them. Listed flat the site reads as unfocused; organised by the buyer's job, it reads as capable.

Local search is the whole funnel

Painting is bought by typing a service and a city into a phone. A site with no page for the city does not exist for that search.

Eighteen areas, and only some can have a page

Naming an area in a footer is not the same as having a page about painting there. The site has to decide which areas earn a page.

Proof has to be visual and real

In this category a stock photograph is worse than none. The buyer wants before and after, in a house that looks like theirs.

The quote is the conversion

A free-quote button that collects only a name and a number produces calls the owner has to screen. A form that asks the job and the budget produces calls he can price.

The organising principle: give every kind of job its own page and every major city its own page, show the work in before-and-after pairs, put the proof where each buyer looks for it, and make the quote request ask the two questions that let the owner price the job before he calls back.

What we built

01 / 07

Job, then place, then proof

Each layer of the site answers the question the previous one raises: do you do my kind of job, do you come to my city, can I see your work, what will it cost. A page per service, a page per major city, a gallery with before-and-after pairs and a quote request that asks the right questions.

02 / 07

A page for each kind of job

The most common failure on a contractor site is one Services page with everything on it. Five pages, one per job, let a buyer read only the one that is theirs, each with a city in the title and the job in the heading. Read as buyer jobs, they stop being a menu and become a map of who the company is for: the home that needs to look like the owner meant it, the building with tenants and a schedule, the façade against Gulf Coast weather.

03 / 07

Prep described before the paint

The exterior page is the most persuasive service page on the site because it describes the part of the job that decides whether paint lasts. A buyer who has had an exterior fail early knows the failure was prep, and the page covers prep in six steps before it describes colour. It also says the company gives written estimates so a buyer can compare prices, and the interior page tells a buyer to get an evaluation before choosing anything, the voice of someone who has seen the alternative.

04 / 07

A page for the city that searched

A homeowner in Katy does not want to read about Galveston. The Katy page names Katy's climate, explains why the paints were chosen to resist water damage, and links to Katy-specific pages for each service. That pattern, a city page that links to a service page for that city, is the scalable one. Local weather named as a reason for a paint choice is a small thing, and exactly what a national template would not say. Eighteen service areas are named in every footer so the buyer can confirm coverage in a line.

07 / 07

A quote that asks the right two questions

The ask is a number, and the site offers four doors. A free-quote popup on every page is fast. The full contact form asks the project type, from commercial exterior to power washing, and an expected budget in eight bands, so the owner arrives at the callback already knowing the job and its size. Call, text and chat buttons serve the buyer who wants a person tonight. A form that qualifies is worth more than a button that only collects.

What's next

Three ways to win the callback

The architecture routes buyers well. The next layer gives more places a page of their own and gives the strongest proof a caption.

  • Give more areas a pageThe Katy pattern, a city page linking to service pages for that city, is the scalable one. Eleven further named areas could follow it, starting with the largest markets.
  • Caption the workForty photographs labelled Before and After become forty chances to say the room, the service and the city, such as cabinets in Sugar Land.
  • Put photographs where the pitch isThe luxury pages describe protection and process, and would earn more of the quote with photographs of the work in those neighbourhoods.

Where does E&M Painting work?

Greater Houston, with eighteen service areas named in the footer from Galveston to The Woodlands. Searches such as commercial painting contractors Texas describe how buyers look, not statewide coverage, and the site publishes no coverage beyond those areas.
