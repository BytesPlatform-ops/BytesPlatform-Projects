The business

The business is broad. The site is where breadth becomes a search footprint, not a blur

On Top Home Services is a veteran-owned home services company in Salem, Oregon. The home page title states the core of the business: “Roof & Gutter Cleaning Salem OR.” Behind it sit sixteen services, seventeen city pages, service-by-city pages beneath each city, a before-and-after gallery, a FAQ and thirty blog articles.

Reduce that to a pressure-washing company and most of the business disappears. The published offer runs from roof cleaning, moss removal and gutter cleaning through house, driveway and window washing to deck and fence installation, remodeling, Christmas light installation and a roof rejuvenation treatment.

The challenge

Sixteen services, seventeen cities, one owner

Every service-plus-town search needs a page, and the pages have to read as one local company rather than a directory. Five pressures shaped the build.

Nobody types “home services”

They type the problem. A site has to be found sixteen different ways and still read as one company when the visitor arrives.

272 possible searches

Sixteen services across seventeen cities is 272 combinations. Building them without the site becoming a maze is the architectural problem.

Exterior cleaning is bought on fear

Will pressure hurt the siding, will the ladder scratch the gutters. The site has to answer before the call or lose the cautious buyer.

Oregon is its own climate

Moss, fir needles, algae on the north wall. A national template cannot say why Willamette Valley roofs fail; a local company has to.

The lead has to arrive sorted

A request for Christmas lights and a request for a kitchen remodel need different follow-up. One message box sends both to the same inbox.

The organising principle: give every service a page, every city a page and every service-in-city a page beneath it; link all three directions; explain the Oregon climate wherever it matters; put the owner and the proof where a homeowner looks; and make the form ask which service.

01 / 07

A page for every service, every city and every pairing

By the time a homeowner calls or submits the form, they have searched the problem with their town in the query, read why it happens in Oregon, looked at a before-and-after, checked who owns the company, and ticked the services they need. The site is built to meet them at each of those six moments.

The grid is the strategy

Most local-service sites choose between service pages and city pages. This one builds both and then the grid between them. A search with a service in it lands on a service page, a search with a town in it lands on a city page, and a search with both lands on a page headed with both: “Roof Cleaning in Salem, Oregon.” Each pairing links sideways to the same service in sixteen other cities and to the other services in the same city.

04 / 07

The explainer that prevents the wrong job

The soft and pressure washing page opens with a warning: “Not every surface should be cleaned the same way.” It explains that soft washing uses garden-hose pressure and a solution that kills mold, mildew, algae and moss at the root, while pressure washing lifts dirt and oil from concrete, brick and stone. A homeowner who has seen a neighbour's siding etched by a rented washer reads that as competence, and it converts the category's most common objection into the reason to hire.

A form that sorts the lead

The contact form asks which of nineteen services, accepts photographs of the problem and promises a reply within 24 hours. Service buttons across the site pre-select the service by URL, so a visitor from the roof cleaning page arrives with roof cleaning already ticked. The company receives a lead it can price, not a message it has to decode, and a Christmas lights request and a kitchen remodel arrive labelled.

What's next

Three ways to widen the footprint

The grid is built. The next layer fills it and gives each town more of its own detail.

  • Write out every pairingRoof rejuvenation has no Salem page yet, and sixteen services across seventeen cities leaves many pairings to write with local detail.
  • Fill the galleryNine project cards for sixteen services leaves most services without one. The filters and town captions are ready for more.
  • Give each city hub its own detailThe Salem service-in-city page names neighbourhoods; the Salem hub itself does not. Neighbourhoods and landmarks on every city page would carry the pattern up a level.

Questions

About this roof cleaning case study

What services does On Top Home Services offer?

Sixteen published services: roof cleaning, moss removal, roof rejuvenation and gutter cleaning; soft and pressure washing, house, driveway, window and vehicle washing; deck and fence installation, interior and exterior remodeling; Christmas light installation, outdoor accent lighting and interior cleaning. It serves Salem and sixteen other Willamette Valley cities.

Does it build decks and fences, or only clean?

Both are published. Decks are built by a named partner “personally vetted and backed by On Top,” fence installation and remodeling have service pages, and permits and Oregon materials are covered in the FAQ. The site publishes no price list for them.

Is a roof pressure washed?

No. The site says so plainly: a roof is soft washed, “never a pressure washer.” Soft washing uses garden-hose pressure and a solution that kills growth at the root, which is why the roof page leads with the explanation.
