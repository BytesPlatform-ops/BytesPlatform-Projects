The business

Three audiences, one front door. The site is where they find their path

Royalty With a Purpose LLC is a cleaning company in Navarre, Florida that also places contract workers with hospitality, retail and facilities businesses along the Emerald Coast. The home page title states the first business plainly: “House Cleaning Services in Navarre | Trusted Professionals.” The hero's two buttons state the second and third: “Request Staff” and “Apply for Work.”

Reduce that to a cleaning website and two of the three audiences disappear. A cleaning site that also recruits can read as a staffing agency to a homeowner, and a staffing page can read as a job board to a hotel manager. The site cannot explain the distinction in a paragraph. It has to be built so that each visitor's first click resolves it.

Who arrives, and why

  • →A cleaning customer, home or business, reading for which service, whether Navarre is covered and how to book.
  • →A business needing workers, reading for what kind of staff, in which towns, and how placement works.
  • →An applicant, reading for what jobs exist and how to put their name in.

The challenge

Three visitors, opposite intentions

A homeowner, a hotel manager and a job-seeker arrive at the same home page wanting a booking, a workforce and an application respectively. Five pressures shaped the build.

Three audiences share one home page

They have nothing in common except the URL. A hero that speaks to one alienates the others; a hero that speaks to all three says nothing.

Cleaning is two markets, not one

A deep clean for a family and a daycare sanitation contract are sold differently and searched differently. One service list cannot carry both without splitting.

Staffing must be explained without taking over

Say too little and it reads as a side-line; say too much and the cleaning customer thinks they are on an agency site.

Local search is the customer funnel

Cleaning is bought by typing a service and a town into a phone, so every customer page carries Navarre.

Three conversions, three forms

A booking wants a date and an address. A staffing request wants a role and a headcount. An application wants availability and a resume. One contact form cannot serve all three.

The organising principle: route every visitor by intention in the first screen, give each intention its own page with its own form or contact route, keep the cleaning offer split into home and business, and never ask one form to do three jobs.

What we built

01 / 07

Route first, then serve

The first two stages are shared and the first click splits the three visitors onto three paths that never cross again. Two hero buttons for the two less common visitors, a Book Now button in the header for the common one, and a page per path with its own form or contact route.

02 / 07

Three buttons in the first screen

The decision to put Request Staff and Apply for Work in the hero and Book Now in the header is the single most important layout choice on the site. The common visitor gets the persistent button. The two less common visitors get the prominent ones, because they are the ones most likely to assume the site is not for them. It is the opposite of what most sites do and the right call here: a job-seeker who cannot find the apply button leaves, while a homeowner will scroll.

Outside the navigationRequest Staff and Apply for Work are reached from the hero, so a customer scanning the menu sees a cleaning site.
The town in the second sentence“Proudly serving Navarre and surrounding areas” tells a homeowner the company is nearby.

03 / 07

Home and business, kept apart

The split into residential and commercial is what keeps a daycare director from reading about move-out cleaning. Eight services sit under two headings, each with one line, each answerable in a scroll, followed by four add-ons. The add-ons are the lines a customer who has hired a cleaner before looks for, because they are the things that were not done last time. Daycare and school cleaning proves the commercial side is real: a general cleaner does not name daycares.

The services, as published

  • 01Residential: deep cleaning, move-in and move-out, recurring, post-renovation.
  • 02Commercial: office, retail and storefront, daycare and school, property management turnovers.
  • 03Add-ons: inside the refrigerator or oven, interior, baseboard detailing, eco-friendly products on request.
04 / 07

The second business, on one page

The Request Staff page states the hinge of the whole site: dependable, vetted workers for hospitality, retail and facility operations. It then does what a staffing conversation does, in order: what kind, which role, how many, where, and how it works. A manager can read the four-step process before deciding to fill in the form. The word vetted tells the business the company stands between it and the worker, which is what a staffing customer is paying for.

05 / 07

Recruiting beside selling, without colliding

An Apply for Work button on a service site is unusual. Here it is visible enough for a job-seeker to find and quiet enough that a customer does not read the business as short-staffed, because the page is reached from the hero and never shares a screen with the cleaning offer. The applicant sees the same ten roles the business can request, so the jobs read as real placements, not a generic careers inbox. A role added to one page belongs on the other.

Mirrored categoriesHospitality, retail and facilities on both pages, so a request and an application are matched by the same word.
Sorted before it arrivesThe role field changes with the category chosen, so the company receives a housekeeper, not a staff member.

07 / 07

Separation, not integration

Every multi-audience site is tempted to build one clever form. This one builds three plain ones, and the three never meet after the first screen: the customer calls or emails, the business fills in a staffing request, the worker fills in an application. A phone number is a tap-to-call button on every page, because for a homeowner who wants a clean this week a call is faster than a form. The business and the worker get forms shaped to their request.

Three conversions

  • 01Customer: phone or email on every page.
  • 02Business: an eleven-field staffing request.
  • 03Worker: an eleven-field application with a resume.

What's next

Three ways to serve each path further

The routing is the hard part and it works. The next layer gives the most common visitor more to act on and sets expectations for the other two.

  • Give the customer a request formA short form with service, home or business, town and preferred date would let a booking arrive routable, alongside the phone.
  • Link services to the blogThe four commercial articles are the natural next click from the business services, so a customer who wants more than one line has somewhere to go.
  • Say what happens after you sendA line on how soon the company replies and which fields are required would set expectations on both the staffing and the application forms.

Questions

About this commercial cleaning case study

Where does Royalty With a Purpose work?

Cleaning is published for Navarre and surrounding areas. The staffing page names five areas along the Emerald Coast: Destin, Navarre, Fort Walton Beach, Pensacola and Santa Rosa County. Searches with other towns describe how buyers look, not wider coverage.

Does it clean restaurants, hotels or floors?

Not as named services. The commercial services are office, retail and storefront, daycare and school cleaning, and property management turnovers. A hospitality staffing category places workers such as housekeepers and room inspectors. No restaurant, hotel contract or floor-care service is published or claimed here.

Is it a cleaning company or a staffing agency?

Both, on one site with three paths. It cleans homes and businesses and also places contract workers. The case study is about keeping those three audiences from colliding on one home page.

Three audiences deserve one clear front door

Bytes & Partners builds websites for local companies whose visitors want opposite things. If your business serves customers, hires workers and sells to other businesses, your first screen is a routing decision. It has to send each visitor down the right path without making the others feel they are on the wrong site, and give each path an action shaped to what that visitor came to do.
