The business

The service is recurring. The site is where the routine starts

DAWG Team USA is a Memphis pet-care business in the Crosstown neighbourhood. The home page states the whole offer in its headline, “Memphis Dog Grooming, Mobile Grooming & Boarding — All in One DAWG Team.” Four services sit behind it: boarding, mobile grooming, in-store grooming and a self-serve dog wash.

“Caring for your dog should not feel complicated” is the brief, and also the claim every local pet business makes, so the site cannot argue it. It has to make simplicity visible: one service per page, the price on the page, the requirements before the booking, and a booking button that works from a phone.

The challenge

Four purchases, one owner, one phone screen

Boarding, mobile grooming, in-store grooming and a self-serve wash are bought for different reasons on different days, often by the same owner. Five pressures shaped the build.

Four services are four purchases

Boarding is bought around a trip. Mobile grooming around a schedule. In-store grooming as a routine. A self-serve wash on a Saturday morning. One page cannot sell all four in the same voice.

Trust is bought for someone who cannot speak

The dog cannot report back. An owner is reading for what happens in the suite, in the van and on the table, and for who the people are.

Price is the first question

Most grooming sites hide price behind a call for a quote. An owner comparing three providers on a phone will leave the one that makes them call.

Requirements arrive too late

Vaccinations, card on file, breed limits, drop-off windows. Discovered at booking, each is a reason to abandon. Discovered on the page, each is a reason to trust.

Booking lives somewhere else

Scheduling is a third-party platform. The handoff has to feel like one business, not a redirect.

The organising principle: ask the owner what their dog needs today, give each answer its own page with the price and the rules on it, put the people and the place where a nervous owner will look, and make every booking button lead somewhere that books.

What we built

01 / 07

Choose, then see, then book

Each layer of the site answers the question the previous one raises: what does my dog need, what does it cost and include, who are these people, how do I book. A service chooser on the home page, one focused page per service with price and requirements, and a booking button on every screen.

02 / 07

Four services, four pages

The home page's four cards are headed with questions, so the owner self-selects in a scroll instead of reading four descriptions to find theirs. The closing section asks the question they came with, “What Does Your Dog Need Today?”, and offers one button per service and one to call. That block is the home page's real navigation. Each service then gets one page of its own in the same shape, so once an owner has read one they know how to read the other three.

The price is on the page

Price is the first question and usually the one left unanswered. Every grooming service is priced by weight across the same five bands, every add-on is priced, boarding is priced by weight, and the wash is a flat rate. The owner arrives at the booking system with the quote already answered, so booking is a confirmation rather than a discovery. 04 / 07

Rules before the booking button

Vaccinations, card on file, drop-off windows and the breeds the van cannot take are all written out before the button. Each would be an abandonment inside a booking flow, and on the page each is a reason to trust. Publishing the limits is the confident move: the owner of a very large breed learns on the page, not on the doorstep, and every other owner reads a business that knows its own limits.

Protection, not frictionRequirements protect the other dogs, and an owner reads them as protection for theirs.
Fees stated up frontA business that publishes its fees is not going to surprise you at the counter.

07 / 07

Three doors, none of them hidden

Every service page ends in the same place: a button to the booking system and a button to call. The phone is a tap-to-call button beside every booking button, and the FAQ tells an unsure owner to call before booking, which is advice that costs the business a booking and is the most persuasive thing a page can do. The wash needs no booking at all, and says so, with hours. The simplest service has the simplest door.

What's next

Three ways to earn the second visit

The structure routes owners well. The next layer helps new owners find the business and gives them more proof once they arrive.

  • Help each service be foundGive every service page its own search title and description so the searches it is written for, such as grooming or boarding in Memphis, can lead to the right page.
  • Show proof on the pageReviews, a gallery of real dogs and short testimonials would give nervous owners evidence beyond the founders' story and the policies.
  • Grow the FAQSix questions is a fraction of what an owner asks: what to bring to boarding, whether they can visit the suite, what happens if the dog is anxious, how matting is priced and whether the van can reach an apartment.
